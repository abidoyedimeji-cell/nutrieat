# Farmers' Market Marketing — Operational Playbook

**Owner:** Marketing / founder  
**Status:** Working plan — validate venue, permissions, product readiness and budget before committing spend  
**Primary brand:** NutriEat  
**Review cadence:** After every market, with a weekly summary during a campaign

This document turns farmers' market activity into a repeatable, measurable channel for introducing NutriEat and validating demand for *My Healthy Cookbook Recipe For You: Breakfast, Lunch, Smoothies and Superfoods*. It assumes NutriEat is promoting a practical, performance-focused cookbook and its early-access / launch journey—not operating a produce stall. If NutriEat later sells food or produce at a market, create a separate food-trading and compliance checklist.

## 1. Objectives and success criteria

Prioritise learning and permission-based audience growth before spending heavily.

1. **Awareness:** explain NutriEat in one sentence and start relevant conversations.
2. **Lead capture:** turn interested visitors into email subscribers using a QR code to the approved early-access page.
3. **Product validation:** learn which benefits, recipes, formats and prices resonate.
4. **Sales:** when checkout and fulfilment are live, convert qualified interest into purchases.
5. **Partnerships:** build relationships with market organisers, local food producers, fitness communities and complementary stalls.

### Pilot scorecard

Set the targets before booking the first event. These are suggested starting targets, not historical performance or guarantees.

| Metric | How to record it | Initial pilot target |
|---|---|---:|
| Meaningful conversations | Count conversations lasting beyond a greeting | 30 per market |
| QR visits | Unique visits to the market-specific URL / analytics campaign | 20 per market |
| New, consented email leads | Confirmed opt-ins attributed to the market | 10 per market |
| Lead conversion | Consented leads ÷ QR visits | 25% |
| Customer insight | Completed short survey or recorded feedback themes | 5 per market |
| Cost per lead | Total attributable market cost ÷ new consented leads | Set a ceiling before booking |
| Sales, when available | Attributed orders and revenue | Establish baseline, then improve |

Do not judge a pilot on email volume alone. Record footfall conditions, weather, pitch position, audience fit, offer and conversations so results can be compared fairly.

## 2. Audience and positioning

### Priority audiences

- Active people and gym-goers interested in structured meals and performance nutrition.
- Busy professionals and students who want practical meal ideas and repeatable routines.
- Home cooks who want ingredient substitutions, shopping lists and less friction planning meals.
- Market visitors already interested in food quality, cooking, local producers and healthy eating.

### Core message

> **NutriEat makes performance-focused eating more practical with structured meals, realistic ingredients and flexible meal rotations.**

Support the message with only confirmed product facts:
- 40 core meals, alongside smoothies, functional snacks, ingredient swaps and three two-week meal plans.
- Macro-aware recipes and practical ingredient guidance.
- PDF (£9.99), hardback (£17.99) and hardback + PDF bundle (£24.99 before shipping), once sales are live and the prices remain current.

Avoid medical promises, guaranteed outcomes, unsupported nutrition claims, and any claim that the cookbook treats or prevents a condition. Do not advertise features that have not shipped.

## 3. Offer and call to action

Use one primary action per event; avoid asking visitors to do too many things.

**Before checkout is live**
- Invite visitors to join early access or a launch list.
- Offer a genuine preview, sample recipe or useful meal-planning tip only if approved and ready.
- Ask one optional research question after sign-up, rather than making the survey a barrier.

**When checkout is live**
- Direct visitors to the product page via QR code.
- State prices and what each edition includes clearly.
- Use a market-specific code only if it is configured, tested and financially approved. Do not promise a discount before it exists.

Suggested spoken opener:  
“Hi! We’re introducing NutriEat, a practical cookbook for people who want more structure around everyday meals and performance nutrition. Would a quick recipe preview be useful?”

Suggested CTA:  
“Scan here to see a sample and join the NutriEat launch list.”

Keep the interaction friendly and brief. Accept “no thanks” immediately; do not pressure visitors or collect details without consent.

## 4. Channel and campaign plan

### Before the market

**2–3 weeks before**
- Research market audience, dates, footfall evidence, pitch fees, application deadlines and organiser rules.
- Confirm permission for promotional activity, QR signage, sampling, photography and any giveaway.
- Book only when the expected audience and cost fit the pilot budget.
- Create a market-specific landing URL or UTM-tagged link; test it on mobile.
- Confirm the landing page, privacy notice, consent language and email confirmation flow.
- Prepare a one-page product sheet and a concise founder / brand introduction.

**1 week before**
- Publish one announcement and one useful content post (e.g. a practical meal-planning tip).
- Ask the organiser whether they will include the stall in their newsletter or social posts.
- Print QR signage with a readable short URL as backup.
- Rehearse the 10-second introduction and answers to price, format, nutrition and availability questions.
- Check weather, transport, accessibility, setup time and cancellation terms.

**Day before**
- Charge devices and power banks; test QR codes on two phones.
- Pack signage, table covering, approved previews, lead-capture backup, pens, water, tape and weather protection.
- Download offline copies of key assets and the lead-counting sheet.
- Confirm the team rota and the single campaign CTA.

### During the market

- Place the main message and QR code at eye level; keep the table uncluttered.
- Start conversations with an open, low-pressure question: “What do you usually find hardest about planning meals?”
- Demonstrate one useful feature or preview; tailor the explanation to the visitor's answer.
- Invite opt-in through the visitor's own device wherever possible.
- Ask permission before taking photos, recording quotes or sharing identifiable testimonials.
- Track conversations, scans, opt-ins, feedback themes and objections in tally form; do not record unnecessary personal details.
- Keep the pitch honest: distinguish live features from planned features.
- Every hour, check signage, QR functionality, stock of printed materials and team energy.

### After the market

**Within 24 hours**
- Reconcile lead counts against the analytics dashboard.
- Send a welcome email only to people who explicitly opted in, with the promised preview or next step.
- Thank the organiser and any partners; request feedback on the fit and potential return dates.
- Record costs, conditions, common questions and what visitors did next.

**Within 72 hours**
- Review landing-page visits, opt-in rate, lead quality, unsubscribe / complaint signals and any sales.
- Group feedback into themes: meal ideas, convenience, nutrition detail, format, price, trust and shopping support.
- Choose one change for the next event; avoid changing several variables at once.
- Follow up with partners only where a clear, mutually useful next step exists.

## 5. Stall and asset checklist

### Essential
- [ ] Approved brand / cookbook one-liner
- [ ] Clear, readable banner or tabletop sign
- [ ] Tested QR code plus short URL
- [ ] Mobile landing page and privacy / consent copy
- [ ] Cookbook cover or approved sample pages
- [ ] Price and format information (only if checkout is live)
- [ ] Market-specific UTM link or campaign code
- [ ] Tally sheet for conversations, scans and feedback
- [ ] Phone, charger / power bank, pens and tape
- [ ] Weather-appropriate setup and accessibility plan

### Optional, subject to permission and budget
- [ ] Printed sample recipe cards
- [ ] Small, approved giveaway with clear terms
- [ ] Partner cross-promotion with a relevant local producer or fitness group
- [ ] Photography or short video, with explicit consent where people are identifiable

If offering food samples, preparing or selling food, or making nutrition / health claims, pause and complete the relevant venue, food-safety, allergen, insurance and legal checks first. This playbook does not authorise food handling or trading.

## 6. Lead capture, consent and data handling

- Use a clear, affirmative, unticked opt-in for marketing emails; sign-up must not be a condition of browsing or receiving general information.
- Explain who is collecting the data, what messages people will receive and how they can unsubscribe.
- Link to the current privacy notice and collect only the fields needed for the stated purpose.
- Do not add people from business cards, conversations, competition entries or third-party lists to marketing emails without an appropriate lawful basis and valid consent where required.
- Keep any giveaway entry separate from optional marketing consent; publish eligibility, closing date, selection method and prize terms.
- Limit access to captured data and follow the documented retention and deletion process.
- Follow applicable UK GDPR, PECR and venue requirements; seek qualified advice if the campaign design is uncertain.

## 7. Measurement and attribution

Use a unique UTM campaign for each event, for example:

`utm_source=farmers_market&utm_medium=offline&utm_campaign=market_<market-slug>_<yyyymm>`

Keep the source and campaign values consistent across QR signage and printed materials. If a QR code is reused, do not reuse the same campaign identifier for a different market.

Record the following for each event:

| Field | Record |
|---|---|
| Market / date | Name, location, date and hours |
| Conditions | Weather, footfall estimate if supplied, pitch location |
| Cost | Pitch fee, travel, print, materials, staff time and other spend |
| Activity | Conversations, QR visits, sign-ups, feedback responses |
| Funnel | Visits → opt-ins → confirmed subscribers → purchases |
| Quality | Audience fit, top questions, objections and useful quotes (with permission) |
| Outcome | Cost per lead, attributable sales, revenue and follow-up actions |
| Decision | Repeat, adjust, test a different market, or stop |

Definitions:
- **Opt-in rate** = new consented leads ÷ unique campaign landing-page visits.
- **Cost per lead** = attributable event costs ÷ new consented leads.
- **Lead-to-sale rate** = attributable purchases ÷ new consented leads over a defined window.
- **Contribution after event cost** = attributable gross contribution − event costs. Do not use revenue alone as proof of profitability.

If attribution is uncertain, label it as uncertain rather than assigning the sale to the market.

## 8. Budget and go / no-go

Use a capped pilot budget. Include all costs—not just the pitch fee—and define the maximum acceptable cost per lead before booking.

**Go if**
- The market audience plausibly overlaps with target readers.
- The organiser permits the intended activity.
- The landing page and consent flow work end-to-end.
- The offer and follow-up are ready.
- The all-in cost is within the approved pilot cap.

**No-go / postpone if**
- Permissions, insurance or trading requirements are unclear.
- The QR journey or consent flow is broken.
- The team cannot follow up with leads promptly.
- The economics depend on unverified footfall or speculative sales.

After 2–3 comparable events, decide whether to repeat, change the offer, test another market, or reallocate effort to online content and partnerships. Small samples are directional, not conclusive.

## 9. Roles and operating rhythm

For a one-person operation, the same person may hold all roles, but each task still needs an owner.

| Workstream | Accountable role | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| Market research, booking and permissions | Founder / event owner | 2–3 weeks before |
| Offer, landing page and campaign tracking | Marketing owner | 1–2 weeks before |
| Creative, signage and kit | Marketing / operations | 1 week before |
| Stall conversations and tallying | Event lead | During event |
| Email follow-up and data hygiene | CRM / marketing owner | Within 24 hours |
| Results review and next decision | Founder / marketing owner | Within 72 hours |

**Weekly review (during active market campaigns):**
1. Compare results against the pilot scorecard.
2. Review the best-performing message and most common objection.
3. Confirm all opt-in follow-ups are complete.
4. Review spend against the cap.
5. Document one experiment for the next market.

## 10. Experiments to run

Test one major variable at a time across comparable events.

- **Message:** “meal planning made practical” vs “performance nutrition made practical”.
- **CTA:** recipe preview vs early-access list.
- **Creative:** cookbook cover-led sign vs benefits-led sign.
- **Conversation opener:** meal-planning challenge vs goal-based opener.
- **Partnership:** organiser newsletter mention vs a relevant local partner promotion.

Do not claim a winner from a handful of interactions. Look for repeated patterns across events and retain notes about audience and conditions.

## 11. Ready-to-use content

### Social announcement

“We’re bringing NutriEat to [market name] on [date]. Come say hello, explore a preview of *My Healthy Cookbook Recipe For You*, and discover practical ways to bring more structure to everyday meals. Find us at [stall/location].”

Publish only after the booking is confirmed. Add the exact time, location and approved CTA.

### Follow-up email outline

**Subject:** Thanks for connecting with NutriEat at [market name]

- Thank the subscriber for opting in.
- Deliver the promised sample or resource.
- Explain NutriEat in one short paragraph.
- Give one clear next step (reply with feedback, explore the site, or view editions if live).
- Include sender identity, privacy link and a working unsubscribe mechanism.

Send only to people who have consented to marketing emails.

### FAQ prompts for the stall team

- Who is the cookbook for?
- What is included, and how is it different from a standard recipe collection?
- Are recipes and nutrition details available to preview?
- Which editions and prices are currently available?
- What is live now versus planned for the future?
- Where can visitors find the privacy notice and contact details?

Answer factually. If a detail is not confirmed, say so and capture it as a follow-up question rather than guessing.

## 12. Decisions to complete before the first booking

- [ ] Select target market(s), dates and geographic area.
- [ ] Confirm whether the activity is promotion-only or includes any food trading / sampling.
- [ ] Set an all-in pilot budget and cost-per-lead ceiling.
- [ ] Confirm launch status and the exact offer / CTA.
- [ ] Approve landing page, sample content, privacy notice and consent flow.
- [ ] Assign event owner and post-event follow-up owner.
- [ ] Set baseline targets and create the campaign tracking sheet.

**Source of truth:** keep product claims, edition details and launch status aligned with `PRODUCT-AND-LAUNCH.md`, `ROADMAP.md` and the live website. Update this playbook after each pilot.
